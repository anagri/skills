# Facilitation

How to run the room so stages 2 and 3 produce something true.

## Silent writing before discussion

The single highest-leverage technique. The sequence is:

1. **Individual reflection** — 5 minutes, no writing on the shared board yet.
2. **Silent brainstorm** — 10 minutes, everyone writes onto the board at once.
3. **Only then, discuss.**

Two failure modes disappear at once. The loudest voice can no longer anchor the session, because the
board is already full before anyone speaks. And the quietest person's input is *on the board* before
any social pressure applies — they no longer have to win airtime to be heard.

Skipping this and "just going around the room" reliably produces a retro that reflects the first
speaker's frame.

**Solo, the technique inverts.** With one attendee there is no dominant voice to defuse, and the
scarce thing is not airtime but accurate recall. So the order flips: collect the artifacts first,
then interview with the evidence in hand — see
[`evidence-sources.md`](./evidence-sources.md#the-sources). The defense against
a self-confirming account moves from *write before you look* to *let the record speak first, and
record where it contradicts you*.

## Psychological safety

State it explicitly at the open, do not assume it:

> This is a safe space. There is no blame here. We are all learning together. What is shared here
> stays here.

**Ground rules** — say them, do not just believe them:

- Assume positive intent
- Criticize ideas, not people
- Everyone participates
- Listen without interrupting
- No side conversations

**Practices that hold it up:**

- Use **anonymous input** for sensitive topics, during org change, or with a newly formed team. Then
  deliberately reduce reliance on it as trust grows — permanent anonymity signals that the room never
  became safe, and it blocks the follow-up questions that turn a complaint into a root cause.
- Keep the feedback balanced. A retro that only collects failures teaches people that participating
  is punishment.
- Acknowledge emotions and concerns rather than routing around them.
- Thank people for vulnerability, specifically and by name.

## Running the discussion

| Step | What you do | Facilitation move |
|---|---|---|
| **Clustering** | Group similar items | Ask "are these the same thing?" and let the group answer |
| **Exploration** | Find root causes | Ask *why* repeatedly — until the answer stops restating the symptom |
| **Solution generation** | Brainstorm fixes | Defer judgment; no criticism during generation |
| **Prioritization** | Choose what to act on | Dot voting — fast, visible, and hard to dominate |

### Questions that work

- Can you help us understand what happened?
- Why do you think this happened?
- How would we prevent this next time?
- What would success look like?
- What would have had to be true for this to go differently?
- Is this a one-off, or the third time we have seen this shape?

The last one is doing quiet pattern-detection across retros, which is exactly what
`<retro-root>/sessions/` exists to make possible — the *Recurring* section of each
`03-insights.md` is where the answer gets written down.

## Facilitator failure modes

- **Answering your own question.** Silence after a question is the group thinking. Wait it out.
- **Letting stage 3 leak into stage 2.** Park interpretations; keep collecting facts.
- **Chasing every item.** The prioritization step exists so that most items are consciously *not*
  discussed. Say so out loud — unspoken triage reads as items being ignored.
- **Closing without restating the decisions.** People leave with different versions of the outcome,
  and the action items quietly become nobody's.
